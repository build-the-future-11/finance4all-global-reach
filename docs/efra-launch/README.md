## Proposed first-member experience

Start with 12–15 interested people and aim for 3–5 introductions that both people find useful. These are planning targets, not current results or guaranteed matches.

1. Confirm a coordinator, working reply inbox, capacity and appropriate arrangements for younger participants.
2. Review the member's stated question, offer, ask, language and availability. Ask only for missing information. Do not infer age or rank people by school prestige.
3. Propose one relevant connection at a time and explain the fit privately to each person.
4. Ask each side for specific permission for the particular introduction and the selected contact details to be shared. Membership opt-in is not blanket introduction permission.
5. Agree a compatible format. A written exchange needs a reply window; a call needs an actual shared slot and named timezone.
6. Introduce only after a human checks both permissions and the current arrangement. Under-18 and age-unspecified direct introductions remain on hold for a separately reviewed, appropriate supervised route.
7. Start with a 25-minute conversation or written equivalent: compare questions, look at one source or obstacle and choose a next step or friendly stop.
8. Ask both people whether the connection was useful before expanding recruitment. A pause is acceptable; no automatic project assignment or announcement subscription follows.

## Optional work, not another programme

- **One question, two countries:** a narrow, sourced comparison with consistent definitions and dates. A data mismatch can be a useful result; do not turn associations into causal claims.
- **Read it with me:** a two-page note on an open paper separating its authors' findings, the participants' interpretation and remaining questions.
- **Can someone else reproduce it?:** reconstruct one public-data calculation or chart with a second-person check, reproduction instructions and a record of discrepancies.

Agree the scope, time budget, source permissions, contribution credit and review before starting. No trading, financial-account access, private financial records or purchased datasets are needed for these starters. No publication, formal peer review, paid placement, expert mentor or institutional endorsement is promised.
